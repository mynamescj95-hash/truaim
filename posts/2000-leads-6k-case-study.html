How We Drove 2,000 Leads on $6K — and What Denver Businesses Can Steal

By Christopher Root · TruAim Marketing · June 18, 2026 · 5 min read

← Back to all posts

One of my favorite campaigns to point to: a paid social program for Hill Country Fishing Camps that generated about 2,000 leads on just $6,000 in spend — roughly $3 a lead — and filled all five of their summer camps to capacity. The business is seasonal and niche, which is exactly why the playbook behind it is so useful. Here's what made it work, and what any Denver small business can borrow.

1. A dead-simple, valuable offer

We didn't try to be clever. The offer was clear and genuinely appealing to the exact parents we wanted: secure your kid's spot at camp. When the offer matches what your audience already wants, your cost-per-lead drops because you're not convincing people — you're just making it easy to say yes.

2. Creative that felt authentic, not corporate

The winning ads used real footage — kids fishing, smiling, the actual experience — not polished stock imagery. People could picture their own child there. That authenticity is what drove the scroll-stopping power and kept costs low.

Authentic, native-feeling creative almost always beats the slick, over-produced version — especially for local and lifestyle businesses.

3. Tight targeting on the right people

We focused on the specific geography and the parent demographic most likely to enroll, then let the platform optimize toward people who took action. No spray-and-pray. For a Denver business, this is the equivalent of focusing on your actual service area and ideal customer instead of "everyone in Colorado."

4. Volume of creative, not a single ad

We didn't ride one ad until it died. We kept fresh creative in rotation so we could swap in winners before fatigue set in. This is the single biggest lever most small businesses ignore — and it's why their results fade after a couple of weeks.

What this means for your business

You don't need a huge budget to get outsized results. You need the fundamentals stacked correctly: a clear offer your audience wants, authentic creative, focused targeting, and a steady supply of fresh ads. Get those right and even a modest spend can fill your calendar.
